Case 01 · Customer and commercial intelligenceReal historical data · tested models

Who should the bank contact first?

A bank has thousands of people to contact, but only enough staff time to reach a fraction. How should it choose where to focus?

Why this matters

Calling everyone costs time and money. Better prioritisation might help a team use limited capacity more intelligently.

Why this analysis

Historical campaign records let us compare a simple statistical model with a more flexible one, then test how well each ranks likely subscribers.

What a manager can do

Explore how the contact limit changes the selected group and test what different cost and revenue assumptions imply.

Analytical approach: predictive modelling looks for patterns in previous outcomes; ranking puts the most promising records first. It does not prove that calling someone causes a sale.

41,188Historical contact records
0.813Ranking score (ROC-AUC), random holdout
66.8%Observed positives among top-ranked 20%
0.520Ranking score in later-period stress test
Validation finding: Although ranking performance was promising on a random holdout, it declined sharply on later observations. This limits claims about prospective usefulness and demonstrates the importance of testing across time.

Dataset: UCI Bank Marketing, Portugal, 2008–2010. Historical contact outcomes, not an active bank client project or evidence of incremental sales uplift.

Which places need closer attention?

An average citywide score can look acceptable while some neighbourhoods experience much greater pressure. How can leaders see the differences?

Why this approach

Can a profitable store still be performing badly?

A branch meets its sales target, yet customers face long queues and frequent stock shortages. Is the apparent success sustainable?

Case 05 · Development and investment intelligenceSimulated decision prototype

Why doesn't spending more always deliver better results?

Two programmes receive similar resources, but one delivers stronger outcomes. What should a funder or public agency investigate?

Why it matters

Knowing the budget alone does not show whether resources are being converted into value.

Why this approach

Compare resources, implementation conditions and observed or assumed outcomes.

Case 06 · Retail customer & revenue intelligenceReal historical data · Interactive analytics

Which customers return, and when does demand rise?

Two years of historical online retail invoice records reveal patterns in purchasing activity, market concentration and seasonality that can inform commercial planning.

Why it matters

Retailers need to distinguish repeat purchasing, seasonal demand peaks and dependence on particular markets.

Why this analysis

Over one million source invoice lines were audited and aggregated into transparent monthly, customer and merchandise summaries.

Decisions supported

Investigate retention opportunities, plan seasonal capacity, and understand geographic and product concentration before intervening.

Methods: transaction analysis, transparent customer grouping by frequency and recency, market concentration, and hypothetical sensitivity testing.

UCI Online Retail II (2009–2011). Real observed aggregates, with cancellations and missing customer IDs explicitly documented. Not a profit estimate, churn model or deployed retailer service.
