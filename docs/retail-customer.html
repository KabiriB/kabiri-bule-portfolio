Case 06 · Customer, Commercial & Transaction Intelligence

Which customers return, and when does demand rise?

A retailer can have a substantial transaction database without knowing where its sales activity is concentrated, how consistently customers return, or which periods place the greatest pressure on trading operations.

This case examines real historical online retail records from two annual worksheets. It reconstructs positive-line sales activity, compares customer purchasing patterns and explores how the resulting evidence could inform retention, merchandising and seasonal planning.

Data source

UCI Online Retail II · Historical UK-based online retail transactions, 2009–2011.

Analytical status

Observed transaction aggregates · Interactive descriptive analytics · Transparent customer group rules.

Important distinction

Real historical data, not current client results. The commercial scenario is hypothetical and does not estimate campaign effects or net profit.

Business problem

A transaction history is not yet a decision tool.

Invoice files tell us what was recorded. Commercial intelligence connects those records to the questions an organisation needs to answer, while documenting the limits of what the data can reveal.

01 / Sales and timing

When does demand concentrate?

Tracking positive-line value by month reveals seasonal peaks and quieter periods that may justify closer attention to inventory, capacity and planning.

02 / Customer behaviour

Who purchases more than once?

Grouping customers by observed invoice frequency and recency makes repeat purchasing visible, without pretending to predict who will return next.

03 / Market exposure

Where is the business concentrated?

Geographic and product-level aggregation identifies dependence on particular markets and merchandise codes, supporting more targeted investigation.

Evidence and interpretation

What the historical records reveal.

These are descriptive findings about the observed transactions. They do not establish why individual customers behaved as they did or what a particular marketing action would cause.

Trading pattern

Strong autumn activity

In the later worksheet, the recorded positive-line value rises markedly in September–November 2011, reaching approximately £1.51 million in November. The final December is only partially observed.

Customer structure

Repeat purchasing is visible

Of 4,338 identified customers with positive purchases, 2,845 had at least two distinct purchasing invoices during the worksheet period. This is an observed repeat-purchase share, not a retention forecast.

Geographic concentration

Heavy UK exposure

Approximately 84.6% of the later period's positive-line value is associated with United Kingdom transactions. This highlights geographic concentration rather than identifying its cause or risk-adjusted impact.

Comparable-month cross-check: Positive-line value for January–November 2011 was approximately 1.8% higher than January–November 2010. Because the worksheets overlap in December 2010 and contain incomplete end months, the annual totals should not be simply added or compared as independent full years. This comparison is not adjusted for inflation, returns or product mix.

Quality, reproducibility & limits

How the analysis was constructed.

Both annual worksheets were streamed and aggregated using a reproducible Python pipeline. Only anonymised aggregate summaries are published in the interactive dashboard; individual invoices and customer identifiers are not exposed.

Unit of analysis
Each source row is an invoice line, not necessarily a distinct order or customer. Distinct invoice counts are calculated from invoice identifiers within each reporting extract.
Positive-line value
Quantity multiplied by Price, restricted to positive-price, positive-quantity lines without a cancellation invoice prefix. It is not net revenue after matched returns or an audited profit measure.
Exclusions
In the 2010–11 extract, 11,805 lines are excluded from the positive-line calculation (including cancellations or nonpositive quantities/prices). These categories overlap and should not be summed independently.
Customer identification
135,080 of 541,910 raw lines (24.9%) lack a customer ID in 2010–11. These lines are included in transaction-level totals when eligible but excluded from individual-customer grouping.
Activity grouping
Customer segments use distinct positive-purchase invoice counts and days since the final observed transaction in each worksheet. These rules are descriptive, chosen for interpretability, and not fitted predictive clusters.
Reporting window
The later extract covers 1 December 2010–9 December 2011. The earlier extract overlaps December 2010. The final December month is incomplete and cannot be compared directly with full months.
