Case 01 · Customer & Commercial Intelligence

Who should the bank contact first?

A bank cannot contact every potential subscriber with equal intensity. The challenge is to use limited campaign capacity intelligently, without confusing a prediction with proof that a call will generate a sale.

This case uses real historical marketing records from a Portuguese bank to investigate whether statistical models can identify groups more likely to subscribe. It then explores what those rankings might mean for a campaign manager under different capacity and cost assumptions.

Business question & analytical design

From a list of contacts to a prioritisation decision.

Marketing resources are scarce. If the bank can reach only 20% of recorded opportunities, random selection may waste capacity. Predictive modelling provides a way to rank historical records according to patterns associated with subscription. The practical question is whether such rankings might help a manager compare allocation choices.

01 / The challenge

Limited contact capacity

Staff time and outreach budgets constrain how many contacts can be pursued. An effective prioritisation approach should explain the trade-off between reach and likely response.

02 / The analytical choice

Compare prediction methods

Logistic regression provides an interpretable benchmark. Gradient boosting tests whether more flexible relationships produce better rankings. Both are evaluated on records excluded from training.

03 / The management implication

Connect rankings to decisions

A capacity simulator shows how different outreach limits change the selected records. Hypothetical cost and contribution inputs illustrate financial trade-offs without claiming measured profit.

Observed performance / Held-out data

Promising ranking, but a serious stability warning.

The flexible model performed slightly better than logistic regression on a randomly held-out test sample. At a 20% contact limit, it selected records accounting for 66.8% of observed positive outcomes in that test sample. However, testing on later records produced very different results.

0.791Logistic regression ROC-AUC · random test
0.813Gradient boosting ROC-AUC · random test
66.8%Subscriptions captured in top-ranked 20% · random test
0.520ROC-AUC · later-period stress test

ROC-AUC is a ranking measure. A result of 0.813 means the model often ranks an observed subscriber above an observed non-subscriber. It does not mean that 81.3% of people would subscribe, nor that the model would create an 81.3% improvement in campaign results.

Interpretation: The large deterioration in later-period performance is central to this case. A model that performs on historical random splits may not remain useful when customer behaviour or economic conditions change. Production use would require stronger forward validation and experimental evidence of incremental response.

Methods and professional judgement

Modelling approach and validation

The banking case compares an interpretable baseline with a more flexible predictive model, evaluates probability reliability and tests whether performance persists beyond the original validation period.

Start with a simpler model

Logistic regression estimates the likelihood of a yes/no result. We used it as a transparent benchmark before trying more complex modelling. Test ROC-AUC: 0.791.

Then test a flexible model

Gradient boosting combines many small decision rules to find more complicated relationships. It was chosen on separate validation data; test ROC-AUC: 0.813.

Check whether probabilities are believable

Calibration asks whether a stated probability matches observed frequency. A recalibration attempt did not improve this test: Brier score 0.0751 before versus 0.0755 after.

Do not peek into the future

Data leakage occurs when a model learns from information unavailable at the actual decision point. We excluded call duration, which would not be known before calling.

Check whether the result survives change

Time-based testing trains on earlier observations and evaluates on later ones. Performance deteriorated sharply, warning against untested real-world deployment.

Separate prediction from impact

A model may predict who subscribes, but that does not tell us whom a call persuades. A randomised study or uplift design is needed to measure the extra effect of contacting people.

Data: UCI Bank Marketing, based on Moro, Cortez & Rita (2014), Decision Support Systems. Test sample n=6,179; overall observed subscribers n=696. This site presents an analytical demonstration, not a deployed bank targeting service.
