Case 05 · Resource & investment intelligence

Why do similar resources produce different outcomes?

Budget size is an incomplete measure of performance. Institutions differ in how well they turn funding, capacity and organisational effort into results. This exploratory comparison shows why decision-makers need to examine the pathway between resources and outcomes.

Explore four simulated system profiles with different input, conversion and outcome scores. The exercise is designed to identify questions about bottlenecks, coordination and implementation—not to claim a measured efficiency ranking.

Reading the evidence

What the comparison can—and cannot—tell us.

The same level of funding can coexist with very different delivery conditions. Effective decision support should separate resource scarcity from bottlenecks in implementation, but doing so credibly requires a sound evidence base.

Limit of inference: All numbers are synthetic. The conversion index is supplied as a scenario value, not derived from inputs or statistically estimated. These scores cannot be compared as money, impact or measured efficiency; no causal attribution or cost-effectiveness conclusion follows.

Methods, provenance & next research step

Transparent about the evidence.

Approach: Comparative systems diagnosis using illustrative composite indicators; not data envelopment analysis, impact evaluation or causal cost-benefit modelling.

Data provenance: A conceptual comparative simulator. Indices and system types are illustrative and cannot establish actual programme efficiency.

Path to an evidence-backed product: A real investment decision product would define a specific intervention and denominator, estimate outcomes, consider selection bias, include costs and uncertainty, and use an appropriate evaluation design where causal claims are required.
