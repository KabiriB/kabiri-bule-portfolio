Executive dashboard architecture

Executive signal architecture

This page sets out a leadership dashboard model designed to help senior teams read organisational health more clearly. The aim is not simply to report what happened, but to show where drift is emerging, where pressure is building, and where attention is likely to matter most.

Design principle

Start with the decisions leadership needs to make

Many dashboards begin with available data and end with a crowded view of metrics. This model begins from a different question: what does a CEO, COO, or Head of Strategy need to understand this week in order to act earlier and more intelligently?

The architecture is therefore organised around four connected pillars: People & Culture, Customers & Market, Operations & Reliability, and Financial Health. The value lies not only in tracking each area, but in seeing how they interact. A service issue may become a customer problem. A people issue may become an operational one. A financial signal may be the lagging expression of something already visible elsewhere.

What makes it useful

Three leadership benefits

  • •Clarity: one disciplined view of whether the organisation is strengthening, holding, or beginning to fray.
  • •Earlier warning: gradual drift becomes easier to detect before it turns into a larger strategic or operational problem.
  • •Shared interpretation: leadership teams can read the same system together instead of relying on disconnected reporting streams.
Best fit for

Where this model is most valuable

  • •Executive committees needing a concise operating view across multiple functions.
  • •Transformation teams aligning measures across business units and reporting layers.
  • •Operations and strategy leaders who want stronger decision visibility, not just more dashboard volume.
