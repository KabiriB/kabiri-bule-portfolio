Data visualisation in 2025: are we just making pretty pictures?

The tools have become remarkably capable. The more important question is whether our charts are helping people understand something more clearly, or simply making complexity look polished.

We are living in a moment when producing visually impressive dashboards is easier than it has ever been. Layouts are smoother, interactions are more fluid, and design tooling continues to improve. None of that is a problem in itself. The problem begins when visual sophistication starts replacing analytical clarity.

A useful visualisation does something quietly demanding: it helps someone see what matters. It makes a relationship, shift, comparison, or imbalance easier to understand than it was before. It respects the viewer’s attention. It creates orientation rather than noise. And it leaves the audience more certain about what they are looking at, not less.

The aesthetic trap appears when teams design primarily to impress rather than to clarify. Complex charts are chosen because they feel more advanced. Dashboards expand because more panels appear to signal more seriousness. Interactivity becomes a substitute for structure rather than a support for understanding. The result is often a polished product that still leaves stakeholders unsure what exactly they are meant to notice, compare, or do.

The discipline I trust is straightforward. Start with the decision, not the chart type. Ask what the audience needs to understand quickly, what signal matters most, and what comparison will genuinely help. If those questions are still unclear, the design work is not finished.

Good visualisation is not opposed to beauty. It simply asks more of it. Hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and restraint should all serve understanding. The aim is not merely to make something attractive. It is to make something legible, trustworthy, and useful under real conditions of limited time and competing attention.

That is a more demanding standard than visual polish alone. But it is also the standard that matters. A strong chart or dashboard should not just look refined. It should help people think better.
